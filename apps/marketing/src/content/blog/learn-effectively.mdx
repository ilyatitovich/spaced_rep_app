Effective learning is less about talent and more about **how you spend attention**.

## Prefer retrieval over re-reading

Ask yourself questions. Cover the answer. Speak it out loud. Write it from memory. Then check.

If you only re-read, you train recognition. If you retrieve, you train usable memory.

## Keep sessions short

A focused 15–20 minutes of due cards beats a two-hour guilt session you dread. Consistency compounds. Exhaustion does not.

Finish while you still have a little energy left. That makes tomorrow’s session easier to start.

## Mix topics, not just one stack

Interleaving related topics (a little vocab, a little concepts) often beats blocking one subject for an hour. Switching feels less fluent — and that difficulty is useful.

## Use honest grades

When a flashcard app asks “did you remember?”, answer truthfully. Inflating “easy” ratings schedules cards too far out and creates false confidence.

## One card, one idea

Bloated cards are hard to grade and hard to remember. Split compound facts. Prefer a clear prompt and a short answer.

## Sleep and revisit

Memory consolidates offline. Studying late and skipping sleep steals the gain. Review again after a night or two — that is where spacing shines.
