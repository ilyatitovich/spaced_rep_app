Your brain is designed to forget. That is not a character flaw — it is a filter. Most of what you see today will not matter next month, so unused traces fade.

## The forgetting curve

When you learn something once, recall is strong for a short window, then drops steeply. Reviewing just before you would forget lifts the curve again. Each successful retrieval makes the next drop slower.

That is the core idea behind spaced repetition: **practice retrieval at expanding intervals**, not massed cramming the night before a test.

## Why cramming feels productive

Cramming creates familiarity. Re-reading notes feels smooth, so you assume you know the material. Smooth recognition is not the same as being able to produce the answer cold the next week.

Retrieval practice — closing the book and trying to recall — feels harder and works better. Spacing spreads those hard retrievals across days so each one strengthens the memory.

## What to do with this

1. Capture what you want to keep as small cards or prompts.
2. Review when the app says a card is due — not when anxiety peaks.
3. Keep sessions short so you return tomorrow.

Spaced repetition is not magic. It is a schedule that respects how memory actually works.
